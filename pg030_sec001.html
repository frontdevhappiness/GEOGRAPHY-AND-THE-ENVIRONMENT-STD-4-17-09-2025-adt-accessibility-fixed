Determining the Cardinal Directions through a compass

A compass is a device used to identify the North direction. There are two types of compasses, namely: digital compass and analog compass. A digital compass uses technology to detect and display the North direction. This type of compass is often integrated into GPS, modern watches, smartphones, and transport vehicles such as cars, boats, and planes, as shown in Figure 4. An analog compass uses a magnetized needle to point to the North.

Figure 4. Picture description. A round compass dial has a black face surrounded by a silver-grey rim. The four cardinal directions are marked in white. North, labelled with the letter N, is at the top. East, labelled with the letter E, is on the right. South, labelled with the letter S, is at the bottom. West, labelled with the letter W, is on the left. The four intercardinal directions are also marked. North-east, labelled with the letters N and E, is in the upper-right. South-east, labelled with the letters S and E, is in the lower-right. South-west, labelled with the letters S and W, is in the lower-left. North-west, labelled with the letters N and W, is in the upper-left. Thin white direction lines meet at a round silver pivot in the centre. A two-coloured needle crosses the centre. Its red pointed end faces slightly east of north, near twenty degrees. Its blue pointed end faces the opposite direction, slightly west of south, near two hundred degrees. A degree scale circles the outer edge. It begins at zero degrees at north and increases clockwise in ten-degree intervals. Ninety degrees is at east, one hundred and eighty degrees is at south, two hundred and seventy degrees is at west, and the scale continues to three hundred and fifty degrees before returning to zero. Short tick marks indicate the spaces between the numbered degrees.
