Geography and the Environment Pupil's Book Standard 4

Maps are essential tools for identifying where people live and where different features are found. Maps can use dot symbols, or colours to present the distribution of people. For example, if you know that many dots are close together on a map, it means many people live in that area, like in big cities. If the dots are spread out, it means fewer people live there, like in the village or small towns.

The distribution of people and objects also can be determined by using Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and other digital tools. This helps us to understand where people are more or less crowded.

Maps also use specific symbols to represent the distribution of different features on the earth’s surface. For example, a house symbol presents the locations of homes, while a school symbol presents the locations of schools. Similarly, maps use different colours to present features. For example, blue might be used for water sources like rivers and lakes, green for areas with forests, and yellow for desert areas.

Generally, you are advised to use the key to know which symbols or colours are used to represent the distribution of objects and people so that you can use the map properly.
